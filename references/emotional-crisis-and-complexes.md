# Emotional Crisis and Complexes

**Use for:** mixed feelings, emotional flooding that is still within the user's capacity, recurring fragments, or “I do not know what I feel.” For immediate danger, dissociation, psychosis, mania, intoxication, or overwhelming trauma activation, stop processing and prioritize grounding and immediate human/local support.

## Work in portions

**Editorial summary.** Hawkins treats emotions as composites that can be met one layer at a time. Do not force a label or a full catharsis. A knot, heat, heaviness, numbness, or “I do not know” is enough. Ask what is most present now; let a manageable portion be felt; pause and reorient if the user becomes less grounded.

Useful hypotheses, always offered as questions:

- anger may cover fear, hurt, shame, or helplessness;
- jealousy may mix desire, fear of loss, and low self-worth;
- grief may include attachment, symbolic loss, and fear of a future without the lost thing;
- apathy may conceal fear of failure, grief, anger, or a learned “I cannot.”

## Recurrent patterns

Recurring feelings can be approached as an invitation to explore a family pattern, expectation, belief, secondary emotion, or attachment—not proof that the method failed. Ask: “When this returns, what is the earliest moment you notice it?” and “What does this feeling seem to promise or prevent?”

Avoid reconstructing an entire history while the user is flooded. Stabilize, name the immediate experience, and return to deeper pattern inquiry only with adequate capacity.
