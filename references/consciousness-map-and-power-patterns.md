# Consciousness Map and Power Patterns

**Use for:** a user who explicitly asks for Hawkins's map or power versus force, or when an active emotional pattern and a possible upward movement materially clarify the guidance. It is a navigation model, not a diagnostic instrument, ranking of human worth, or a basis for remote calibration.

This page supplies orientation only. When guiding an actual transition, use [transcending-levels-process.md](transcending-levels-process.md) and read the matching *Transcending the Levels of Consciousness* chapter; a map label does not explain how its limitation is relinquished.

## Compact map

| Hawkins level | Practical navigation cue |
| --- | --- |
| Shame | dignity and the right to support |
| Guilt | accountability, correction, forgiveness |
| Apathy | one possible choice or action |
| Grief | acceptance, nonattachment, renewed presence |
| Fear | courage |
| Desire | preference without dependency |
| Anger | constructive power and assertiveness |
| Pride | humility, gratitude, stable self-respect |
| Courage | agency and willingness to face reality |
| Neutrality | flexibility and nonattachment |
| Willingness | responsibility and growth |
| Acceptance | discernment and harmony |
| Love / Joy / Peace | connectedness, gratitude, stillness |

> “The level of Courage (200) is the critical point that marks the shift from negative to positive energy.”

— *Letting Go*, Chapter 3, `sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/03-the-anatomy-of-emotions.md`

## Direction of movement

Within Hawkins's framework, the practical purpose of the map is directional: relinquishing lower emotional programs makes progressively freer and more life-supportive patterns available. Do not use the table merely to describe where the user seems to be. After sufficient surrender, invite the user to test a higher context and continue while the movement remains honest.

The movement need not be one adjacent level at a time. Fear can open directly into courage, acceptance, reason, or compassion; anger can reveal courage and then willingness; grief can open into acceptance, gratitude, or love. Use the user's actual shift rather than forcing a preset sequence. A cognitively attractive answer is not evidence that the emotional charge has transcended.

## Power and force

In Hawkins's doctrine, force is coercive, oppositional, and draining; power is associated with truth, integrity, and life-supportive action. Use that contrast to ask whether the user is acting from compulsion or choice. It must not become an excuse to avoid firm action, protective force, law, boundaries, or legitimate conflict resolution.

> “The decisive level of 200 is the fulcrum that divides the general areas of force (or falsehood) from power (or truth).”

— *Power vs. Force*, Chapter 4, `sources/Power-vs-Force/04-part1-levels-of-human-consciousness.md`

## Attitude contrasts

Chapter 9 offers pairs as a reflection tool, not labels to apply to the user: **allowing / controlling**, **accepting / rejecting**, **flexible / rigid**, **forgiving / condemning**, **choosing-to / having-to**, **confident / arrogant**, and **confronting / harassing**. Ask what is active and what more humane alternative is available; never use the pairs to shame or dominate.

— *Power vs. Force*, Chapter 9, `sources/Power-vs-Force/09-part2-power-patterns-in-human-attitudes.md`

## Non-negotiable limits

- Never infer or assign an exact numeric calibration from text.
- Never claim to muscle-test a person, event, teacher, object, or statement remotely.
- Never call a person “low consciousness” or treat an emotion as their identity.
- A person may function differently across contexts; use the map only as a tentative way to notice the active pattern and a humane next movement.
- Do not stop at labeling or normalizing a lower pattern when the user is asking for transformation and has capacity for an upward inquiry.
- Attribute metaphysical claims to Hawkins's framework.
