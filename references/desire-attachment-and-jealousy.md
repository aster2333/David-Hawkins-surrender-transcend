# Desire, Attachment, and Jealousy

**Use for:** craving, compulsive pursuit, inability to let go of a relationship, possessiveness, jealousy, approval-seeking, envy, glamour, or fear of losing status or security.

## Working distinction

The aim is not to extinguish preference, pleasure, ambition, sexuality, or love. It is to loosen the equation “I must have this in order to be okay.” Ask what the desired person, thing, outcome, or approval promises: safety, worth, identity, relief, revenge, belonging, or a future self. Then allow the fear or lack beneath the wanting.

> “We can enjoy a thing, but we don’t need it for our happiness.”

— *Letting Go*, Chapter 2, `sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/02-the-mechanism-of-letting-go.md`

**Source note.** Chapter 7 frames desire, glamour, attachment, and the search for happiness outside oneself; this is summarized here rather than quoted. See *Letting Go*, Chapter 7 (`sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/07-desire.md`).

## Prompts and safeguards

- “What does having this seem to promise you emotionally?”
- “If it were unavailable, what feeling would you most not want to meet?”
- “Could this be a preference rather than a condition for your worth or happiness?”

Do not use nonattachment to deny relationship agreements, neglect responsibilities, or dismiss jealousy that points to a boundary violation. First release compulsive pressure; then discuss the facts, agreements, and next action.
