# Relationships and Work

**Use for:** resentment toward a partner, family member, colleague, manager, or child; jealousy; controlling or changing another person; approval-seeking; work stress, success pressure, and practical action. When emotional visibility or empathy is central, also use [relational-emotion-bridge.md](relational-emotion-bridge.md).

## Process before action, then action

First acknowledge the emotion without suppression or attack. Ask what expectation, threat, or desired influence is alive: “What do you want this anger to make them understand or do?” Then distinguish the internal charge from the external facts. Release does not mean no boundary, no performance conversation, no report, or no exit plan.

After surrender, actively test how a higher state changes the relationship: courage may support honest expression; neutrality may loosen the need to control the outcome; willingness may support listening or repair; acceptance may allow clear facts and boundaries; reason may organize the problem; love may add care without erasing discernment. Do not require the user to communicate merely because another person is involved.

> “That purpose is directly related to achieving an effect on the other person’s feelings and, by doing so, to influence their feelings toward ourselves and to fulfill our own inner goals.”

— *Letting Go*, Chapter 18, “Relationships,” `sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/18-relationships.md`

**Editorial boundary:** Chapter 18 also advances energetic and psychic-influence claims. Treat those as Hawkins's metaphysical framework, not verified facts; use the practical inquiry about hoped-for influence without claiming telepathy or causation.

> “Expectations are subtle pressures on the other person, who will then unconsciously resist.”

— *Letting Go*, Chapter 21, “Relationships,” `sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/21-questions-and-answers.md`

> “Let go of judging, blaming, and controlling the other. Let go of expecting them to be different than they are.”

— *Letting Go*, Chapter 21, “Relationships,” `sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/21-questions-and-answers.md`

## Boundaries

Do not pressure a user into reconciliation, forgiveness on demand, contact with an ex, silence about harm, or remaining with someone abusive. Releasing resentment can coexist with noncontact, documentation, a clear request, workplace escalation, legal/medical advice, or safety planning.

## Work and achievement

Explore whether the user is being driven by fear, approval, competition, or inadequacy. Do not tell them to abandon a goal; invite a higher motive such as enjoyment, contribution, learning, craftsmanship, or service.

> “When a lower motivation has been let go, the mind automatically replaces it with a higher feeling and a higher motivation.”

— *Letting Go*, Chapter 21, “The Mechanism,” `sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/21-questions-and-answers.md`

Use this as Hawkins's teaching, not as a guarantee of career outcome.
