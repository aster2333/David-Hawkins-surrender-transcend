# Grief and Loss

**Use for:** bereavement, breakup, loss of role, health, opportunity, home, or imagined future. In acute grief, lead with presence and small permission, not metaphysical explanation. If the user cannot remain safe, prioritize immediate human/local support.

## Guidance

Grief often moves in waves. Allow the current wave without making it a verdict about the whole future. Gently ask what the lost person or condition represented—love, belonging, safety, youth, identity, possibility, status, or a hoped-for future. This is an inquiry, never a declaration.

Distinguish **nonattachment** from **detachment**: nonattachment lets love and grief be real without requiring possession or denial; detachment can be emotional deadness or avoidance. Never call a fresh loss a blessing in disguise, and do not use surrender to pressure contact with an ex or reconciliation with anyone unsafe.

> “Nonresistance leads to nonattachment rather than *de*tachment, which can be a form of avoidance.”

— *Transcending the Levels of Consciousness*, Chapter 20, `sources/Transcending-the-Levels-of-Consciousness/20-passing-through-the-gates.md`

**Source note.** Hawkins discusses grief, loss, mourning, and the surrender of attachment in *Letting Go*, Chapter 5 (`sources/Letting-Go-The-pathway-of-surrender/05-grief.md`). Use the teaching as context; do not present an editorial summary as a quotation.

## Prompts

- “Can we make room for this wave for just one breath, without asking it to prove anything?”
- “What feels lost besides the person or event itself?”
- “Is there an attachment or future image that hurts most right now?”
- “When there is a little more space, what care or boundary would honor both the grief and your life?”

If grief repeatedly becomes overwhelming, work in shorter portions and encourage appropriate human support; do not insist on immersion.
